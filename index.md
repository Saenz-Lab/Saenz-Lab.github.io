<section class="hero">
  <h1>Engineering membrane phenotypes in minimal and synthetic cells</h1>
  <p>
    The Saenz Lab studies how lipid membranes give rise to physical phenotypes that shape
    cellular function. We combine lipid biochemistry, membrane biophysics, and synthetic
    genomics to understand how membrane phenotypes are encoded, regulated, and
    experimentally engineered.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="grid">
  <div class="card half">
    <h2>Membrane phenotypes</h2>
    <p>
      Cellular membranes exhibit measurable physical properties such as order,
      permeability, and stability. We study how these properties emerge from lipid
      composition and organization, and how they constrain cellular function.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="card half">
    <h2>Lipidomes as control spaces</h2>
    <p>
      Lipidomes are not static inventories but adaptable, genetically encoded design
      spaces. We investigate how lipid composition can be tuned to modulate membrane
      physical state and functional robustness.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="grid">
  <div class="card third">
    <h2>Minimal and synthetic cells</h2>
    <p>
      Genomically minimal cells provide reduced experimental systems in which membrane
      complexity can be systematically removed and rebuilt, revealing design principles
      that are obscured in more complex organisms.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="card third">
    <h2>Regulation and homeostasis</h2>
    <p>
      We explore how cells sense and regulate membrane physical state, and how membrane
      phenotypes are coupled to lipid synthesis, metabolism, and gene regulation.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="card third">
    <h2>RNA–lipid interactions</h2>
    <p>
      RNA can interact directly with lipid membranes. We study how membrane composition
      and physical state influence RNA structure, activity, and stability, opening new
      avenues for membrane-sensitive regulation.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>
